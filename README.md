# RE6CabbyCodes

A cheat panel for **Resident Evil 6** on Steam (app `221040`). Pause the game and a panel appears beside the pause
screen.

Pause with the game's own **Pause** key: Start on a gamepad; on the keyboard, the key the game's control settings bind
to Pause (rebind it there if you like). **Esc** opens a different menu, where the panel does not appear.

## What it does

**Cheats**
- **God mode** - you take no damage, and your health is held at its maximum. A hit on you does no damage (you still
  flinch), and the enemies' grabs and blows take nothing either, nor do they put you down. A death the game scripts
  without a hit (a fall, a missed QTE) is left to it. The vehicle you ride - Ada's helicopter, Chris's jet, a
  snowmobile, a bike or a jeep - takes no damage either while you are on it, and is repaired as you switch this on.
  It covers every character played on this computer, a second player on the same screen too.
- **Partner takes no damage** - the same for your partner while the computer plays them. A partner played by another
  person over the network is left to their game.
- **Infinite stamina** - your combat gauge (the blocks beside your health) never runs down: melee moves, quick shots
  and every other move that costs blocks take none, so none is ever held back for want of them. If the game empties
  the gauge itself (as you go down), it is full again as soon as you are back on your feet. Under water the same
  gauge is your air - that is Infinite air's. It covers every character played on this computer.
- **Infinite air** - under water your air never runs out: the air gauge that shows while you swim under water stays
  full, so you never drown (the dives where the game counts your breath: Leon's chapter 3 and Ada's chapter 1). God
  mode cannot save you from drowning; this can. On land the same gauge is your combat gauge - that is Infinite
  stamina's. It covers every character played on this computer; a partner the computer plays never runs short of air
  in the game anyway.
- **One hit kills** - any hit by you or your partner that does damage takes all of an enemy's health, so it dies
  through the game's own death. An enemy the game keeps alive for now (a boss between phases, one that may not die
  yet, or one only the stage's own script ends - the water's Brzak, Ustanak, Haos before its last fight) keeps its
  life - but each of its weak spots, parts and counters (mutated Deborah's cores, on the mine cart too; Brzak's tongue;
  Rasklapanje's limbs; Ustanak's knockdowns) gives at one hit, and it staggers as from the biggest hit, so its fight
  still moves on a shot at a time. A boss whose fight goes in stages loses a stage at a hit. The robots that roll
  along the floor and blow up (Leon's chapter 4) go off at your first hit, as from a shot that destroys them outright.
  Damage the enemies do to each other, and to the people you protect, is left as it is.
- **Automatic QTEs** - the quick time events play themselves: a grab is broken free of, a button mashed, a stick
  waggled or turned (a crank winds at full speed), two buttons pressed together, a dodge or a counter taken - as soon
  as its prompt comes up, without it being drawn. A press in time waits for its best moment and is taken then. The
  scenes the game itself makes automatic for its own option take that path too (your options are not changed).
  Prompts you choose to act on - doors, items, cover, melee moves, helping your partner - are left to you.
- **Infinite ammo** - every weapon of yours gets the game's own infinite ammo: firing uses no rounds, so there is
  nothing to reload, and a thrown grenade stays in your inventory. The helicopter Ada flies keeps all its missiles
  too (any it has fired come back as you switch this on). Health tablets are still used up as usual.
- **Steady aim** - your aim holds still: no sway while you aim or look through a scope, no kick when you fire. Your
  shots go where the reticle is (a shotgun keeps its spread of pellets). Through a scope the view stands still whenever
  you are not moving your aim - the rifle's breathing and its kick at a shot don't move it - and follows your mouse or
  stick as usual.

**Skill points**
- **Infinite skill points** - buying a skill (or its next level) on the skill screen takes no skill points; picking
  skill points up still adds to them.
- A **skill points** editor: your profile's skill points, changed at once (the game keeps them with your profile the
  next time it saves).

**Countdown**
- **Freeze countdown timer** - the countdown on screen stands still, so time never runs out: a stage's countdown in
  the campaigns, and the Mercenaries' clock (its time bonuses still add to it). It runs on from there the moment you
  switch it off. Offline only.
- While a countdown is on screen, a **time left** editor sets its seconds (up to 99:59, the most the game's clock
  shows).

**The Mercenaries** - a section of their own, shown only during a match of The Mercenaries or No Mercy, with the
match's score, combo, kills and time left beside the switches. They act in a game of your own, not online.
- **Endless combo** - a combo never runs out of time: every kill counts as the next one in it however long since the
  last, so its bonus keeps climbing. The game puts what a combo has earned onto your score only once the combo is
  over; with this on it goes on as it is earned instead.
- **Combo Time always on** - Combo Time (what its hourglass gives: every kill's combo bonus at least 1,000) runs from
  the moment you switch it on and never runs out.
- **Kill enemies as they appear** - every enemy dies the moment it is in play, wherever it is: the game's own death,
  and the kill scores as one of your hand-to-hand kills - its points, the combo, the stage's kills and the time a
  hand-to-hand kill puts on the clock. Each enemy is hit until it is dead, whatever its armour or its own damage makes
  of a hit (one far off may wait until it wakes); one the game keeps alive by its own rules (Rasklapanje never loses
  its last point) is hit again and again, on another part of it each time. While it is on, a body vanishes the moment
  its kill counts (no death animation, no dissolve), and the stage sends its next enemy whenever there is room (it
  sends only while fewer than its limit are about, and counts the bodies), without its own wait between enemies (as
  much as 2 s); it sends from its first area wherever you stand (the maps that send by where you are), and each of its
  waves also sends what the waves before it left unsent (on Steel Beast the stage stopped at 89 kills without it). It
  waits for the clock to start and stops when time is up. It lasts one match: it switches itself off as the match
  ends - switch it on again in the pause of the next.

**Results** (the chapter's, as the results screen shows them; in the campaigns)
- **Freeze play time** - the chapter's clock, which its rank goes by, stands still.
- **Deaths don't count** and **Misses don't count** (a shot that misses is not counted, so the accuracy stays at
  100%).
- An editor for the chapter's time (one clock: the game counts the same time for every character) and editors for
  your character's deaths, kills, shots and hits.

**Inventory** - for each character in play:
- each item it holds - its weapons, its health tablets, and what is in its case (ammo, herbs, grenades, sprays) -
  with its count (a gun's loaded rounds, up to its capacity, for each of its loads where it has several - the Wing
  Shooter keeps one for One Gun and one for Two Guns, both hands; a stack, up to what one slot holds): type a new one
  and press **Apply** (or **Undo**), or **Apply all** the counts you typed;
- **x** throws an item in the case away, as the game's own Discard does; tick items (or **All**) to **Throw away**
  them together (never the one in hand);
- **Add** - ammo, grenades and recovery items under tabs, with a search box. The ammo listed is what that
  character's own weapons load (a Grenade Launcher's three kinds of rounds, a Crossbow's two arrows, ...). Weapons are
  given on Records > WEAPONS (below). An item goes in as a pickup does: onto the stacks of it you have first, the rest
  into free slots. A grenade or spray the stage has not loaded (nobody there has one) is loaded first by the game's
  own loader, the moment you pick it in the list. Offline only.

**Records** - windows of their own beside the game's screens in the menus (Special Features), each opening by itself
on its view. Each change shows on the game's screen at once, and your profile is saved right away, by the game's own
save.
- **Chapters completed** - on Records > PROFILES > Completed: for each character, difficulty and chapter, pick whether
  it is completed and at which rank (S to E: the mark the grid shows), or pick a whole row, a character or everyone at
  once, then **Apply all**. Under each completed chapter is its best time (h:mm:ss, as Chapter Select shows it):
  type another, or one beside the rank for a whole character or everyone at once. A chapter completed here without
  a time gets 19:59:59, the game's own "no time"; a real clear replaces any time it beats. A chapter newly completed
  also gets what clearing it gives: its dog tag template, its
  achievement, and what completing a campaign unlocks (Ada's campaign once Leon's, Chris' and Jake's are complete;
  "Normal Is Good" and the like once every chapter is completed on that difficulty or a harder one). Taking a
  completion back leaves those as they are.
- **Medals** - on Records > MEDALS: every medal's count (0 to 99), under a tab for each category the game shows: type
  a count and **Apply**, or one count for every medal listed (**Type in**, then **Apply all**).
- **Weapons** - on Records > WEAPONS, two things in one grid:
  - **The page's "? ? ?"**: the page names a weapon (or a hand-to-hand move) only once you have used it, except each
    character's starting weapons, the tablets and the spray, which it always names. Tick a row's **Page** box (or
    **Name every row**) and it is named, with one use counted (Times Used 1). Untick it to make it "? ? ?" again,
    though not once you have really used it more than that.
  - **Each character's weapons**: every weapon a campaign hands out along the way (the Shotgun, Lightning Hawk,
    Grenade Launcher, Bear Commander and so on), with a tick for each character whose campaign has it. Many are
    shared (the Shotgun is Leon's, Helena's, Jake's and Sherry's), so each character has its own tick and a count
    per character shows who is missing what. Tick one (or a character's **All**, a weapon's **All**, or **Give every
    weapon**) and it goes into that character's saved inventory with a full magazine: Continue, New Game and Chapter
    Select start with it, as with a weapon you found. Untick it to take it out (its loaded ammo goes with it). A
    partner played by the game is handed the story's weapons whatever is saved.
- **Enemies** - on Records > ENEMIES: the page names an enemy only once you have defeated one (or one has defeated
  you), except J'avo and Zombie, which it always names. Each of the others is listed with its counts; tick a "? ? ?"
  one (or **Reveal all**) and it is named, with one kill counted (Enemies Defeated 1). An enemy already named keeps
  its counts, and a revealed one stays revealed.
- **Emblems** - on Dog Tags > EMBLEM: every emblem with the achievement it belongs to and what earns it. **Award**
  (or tick several, or **Tick all locked**) gives the achievement through the game's own call, as earning it does: the
  emblem unlocks, and so does the achievement on Steam - Steam does not take an achievement back.
- **Titles** - on Dog Tags > TITLE: every title with what earns it and how far you are; **Next** awards the next level,
  **All** every level (or tick several and **Award to the top**). An award also brings the title's count up to what it
  needs (never down), as earning it would: the game shows what earns a title, and its page of the list, only once its
  count has started. A level-4 title also gives "Rising Up", and the tenth title held "Titular Achievement", as the
  game's own check does. Titles awarded by an earlier build show "? ? ?" in the game: **Bring their counts up** fixes
  them.

**Skills** - a window of its own beside the game's skill screen (the campaigns' skills - wherever the game shows it:
from the menus, or between chapters), opening by itself.
- **Unlock** the skills the game sells only once you have met what it asks: J'avo Killer and Zombie Hunter (kill 30),
  Grenade Power-Up and the weapon masters (kills with that kind of weapon), and the infinite ammo skills (every
  campaign completed). Each shows what the game asks and how far you are. Tick one (or **Unlock all**) and the skill
  screen sells it at once - "? ? ?" becomes the skill, with its price; you buy it there with your skill points as
  usual. Nothing is bought here, and your skill points stay as they are. Untick one to lock it again (not one you have
  bought, nor one whose requirement you have met: the game sells those itself). A skill's next level is sold once you
  own the one before, as always.
- Your profile keeps the change when you leave the skill screen, as it keeps your purchases.

**The Mercenaries' unlocks** - a window of its own beside the stage select and the character select of The
Mercenaries and The Mercenaries No Mercy, opening by itself.
- **Stages** - the mode's ten stages. Tick one (or **Open every stage**) and the stage select lets you pick it at
  once; untick it to close it again. Each mode has its own: the first three are always open, and the game itself opens
  each of the others after an A rank or better on the stage above it.
- **Characters and costumes** - each character with its Default and Costumes 1 to 3, then Carla and the Agent; one
  set for both modes. Tick one (or a character's **All**, or **Unlock every costume**) and the character select offers
  it, with the Collection file the game gives with that costume; untick it to lock it again. Leon's, Chris' and Jake's
  Default and the Agent are always there, and so are Coach, Nick, Ellis and Rochelle in No Mercy. In the game,
  Costumes 2 and 3 came only from online event rewards: this is the way to them now.
- Your profile is saved right away, by the game's own save.

**F7** hides or shows the panel at the pause; it is back by itself the next time you pause. In play, F7 opens the
panel over the game. Beside a records screen, the skill screen or a Mercenaries select screen F7 hides or shows its
window (it is back when the screen changes). The
cheat switches are remembered between starts, and so is each window's place, size and what you folded in it. Numbers
in a game are changed only while the game is paused.

## Install

Works the same on Windows and on Linux (Proton), with no launch options.

1. Open the game's folder (in Steam: right-click the game > Manage > Browse local files).
2. Rename the game's own **steam_api.dll** to **steam_api_orig.dll** (the mod passes the game's Steam calls on to it).
3. Copy this mod's **steam_api.dll** in beside **BH6.exe**.

The mod leaves the file names other mods use free: ReShade and DXVK (`d3d9.dll`), ASI loaders (`dinput8.dll`,
`version.dll`, `winmm.dll`) and controller wrappers (`xinput1_3.dll`) all work beside it.

Steam's **Verify integrity of game files** puts the game's own steam_api.dll back over the mod's: copy the mod's in
again afterwards (steam_api_orig.dll stays where it is).

To uninstall: delete the mod's steam_api.dll and rename steam_api_orig.dll back to steam_api.dll.

It is made for the current Steam version of the game (build 10112070). On another version the mod says so in the
panel and leaves the game alone.

### If it does not work

The mod says why - in a message box as the game starts (such as a missing steam_api_orig.dll, with the steps to fix
it), or in the panel. Otherwise send **RE6CabbyCodes.log** and **RE6CabbyCodes.prev.log** from the game's folder.
When the game closes by itself, the log's last lines say how it ended, or where it crashed.

## Please read before using

- This is a first version: everything it uses was found in the game's own code and checked against it, but it has
  not been played with yet. Expect rough edges, and report them with the log.
- The cheats act in a game of your own; online, the other player's side is left to their game.
- Awarding an emblem, or completing chapters and titles that earn achievements, unlocks those achievements on your
  Steam account, as earning them in the game does.
- The game saves to Steam Cloud by itself: what you change is kept by its next save.

## Config

`RE6CabbyCodes.ini` beside the DLL (written with every option documented on the first start):

```ini
ToggleKey               = 0x76  ; the key that hides/shows the panel (0x76 = F7)
GodMode                 = 0     ; each cheat's state when the game starts - the panel writes changes back here
PartnerGodMode          = 0
OneHitKills             = 0
InfiniteAmmo            = 0
SteadyAim               = 0
AutoQTE                 = 0
InfiniteSkillPoints     = 0
FreezeCountdown         = 0
FreezePlaytime          = 0
NoDeathCount            = 0
NoMissCount             = 0
EndlessCombo            = 0
ComboTimeAlwaysOn       = 0
KillEnemiesAsTheyAppear = 0
```

Beside it the mod keeps `RE6CabbyCodes.log` (what it found and did) and `RE6CabbyCodes.imgui.ini` (the window's place
and size, and what is folded).

## Building from source

Cross-compiled on Linux with mingw-w64:

```sh
cp config.mk.example config.mk   # set GAME_DIR to your game folder
make                             # build/steam_api.dll
make install                     # installs the mod (keeps the game's steam_api.dll as steam_api_orig.dll)
make uninstall                   # removes it (puts the game's steam_api.dll back)
```

`tools/` has the reverse-engineering helpers (the game's class records and their fields, the signatures) and the
generator of the Steam DLL's export list.
